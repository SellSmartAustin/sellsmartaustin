Your Real Options

Three real options for a tired landlord

1

Sell as-is with tenants in place

A cash sale to an investor with the lease honored and the deposit credited to the buyer at closing. No make-ready, no vacancy, no parade of open-market showings. Typically it's one scheduled walkthrough, coordinated with your tenant under the lease terms. The tradeoff is price: an occupied as-is sale usually closes below what a fixed-up vacant listing would bring. I'll show you that gap in writing so you can decide if speed and simplicity are worth it.

2

Wait for vacancy and list for top dollar

When the lease ends, you make the property ready and list it on the open market. Retail buyers usually pay more than investors, but be honest about the cost of getting there: paint, flooring, repairs, plus every month of mortgage, taxes, and insurance you carry with no rent coming in. Sometimes the higher price survives that math and sometimes it doesn't. I'll run the numbers before you commit either way.

3

Keep the rental and fix the real problem

If the property cash flows and the real problem is tenant calls and turnovers, selling might be the wrong move. A good property manager takes the day-to-day off your plate for a share of the rent. That path doesn't involve me at all, and I'll tell you if it's your best one. Plenty of tired landlords need a manager, not a buyer.

The Mechanics

How selling a tenant-occupied rental actually works

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. In Texas a lease runs with the property, so a sale doesn't cancel it, and the security deposit transfers to the buyer. For any lease dispute or tenant question beyond the basics, talk to a landlord-tenant attorney. Here's what matters most for rental owners selling across the Austin metro, including Travis and Williamson counties.

The lease goes with the house

A Texas lease survives the sale. The buyer steps into your shoes as landlord, honors the existing terms, and gets the deposit credited at closing. Your tenant's rent and lease dates don't change because you sold.

Occupied price vs vacant price

An investor buying an occupied rental prices it on rent and condition. A retail buyer pays for a move-in ready house. The vacant listing usually wins on price, but only after make-ready costs and months of carrying an empty property. Compare nets, not prices.

1031 exchange if you're not done investing

Selling one rental to buy another? A 1031 exchange can defer capital gains taxes. The rules and deadlines are strict, so confirm the details with your CPA before you list. If it fits your plans, I'll write the contract to accommodate the exchange and coordinate timing with your CPA and qualified intermediary.

Before You Reach Out

Straight answers to the usual questions.

Can I sell my rental property with tenants in it in Texas?
Yes. In Texas the lease runs with the property, so you can sell anytime and the buyer takes over as landlord under the existing terms. Investors like that the property earns from day one. You don't need to wait for the lease to end, and you shouldn't ask a tenant to leave outside the lease terms. For lease questions, talk to a landlord-tenant attorney.
Will a tenant-occupied house sell for less in Austin?
Usually the occupied price is lower than what a fixed-up vacant listing would bring. But compare what you keep, not the sale price. A vacant listing carries make-ready costs, months of mortgage and taxes with no rent coming in, and commission and closing costs on the higher price. Both paths have transaction costs, and my net sheet shows every one on each side so the comparison is apples to apples. Sometimes the occupied sale nets nearly as much with none of the wait. I'll put both numbers in front of you.
What happens to the security deposit when I sell a rental?
It's credited to the buyer at closing, usually on the settlement statement. But under Texas law you stay on the hook to your tenant until the new owner gives them a signed statement taking responsibility for the deposit, so make that signed acknowledgment part of closing. Handled correctly, with that acknowledgment in place, your tenant deals with one landlord, not two.
Should I sell my rental or just hire a property manager?
Depends on why you're tired. If the property cash flows and the problem is late-night calls and turnover work, a property manager may fix it for a share of the rent, and that answer doesn't involve me. If the property loses money, needs major work, or you want the equity for something else, selling is worth pricing out. I'll tell you which one your numbers point to.
Can I use a 1031 exchange when I sell my rental property?
Possibly. A 1031 exchange lets you defer capital gains taxes by moving the sale proceeds into another investment property, with a qualified intermediary holding the funds so they never pass through your hands. The IRS deadlines are strict: 45 days to identify the replacement property, 180 to close. It only fits if you're staying an investor rather than cashing out. I'm not a tax advisor, so confirm your situation with a CPA before you commit to a sale structure.
